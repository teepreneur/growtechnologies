01 Power

The sun goes on top

Every module carries its own ~450 W solar panel as a roof. The power source uses no extra ground, and its shade keeps the top tier cooler.

~1,350 Wh / day*Tilt ~12°450 W
02 Storage

All the power in one small cabinet

A lockable cabinet on the end of the frame holds the 24 V lithium battery, the charge controller and the controller board. The yellow dots show energy flowing from the panel into the battery and out to the farm.

24 V DC2.56 kWhNo inverter
03 Water

Water that never stops moving

The pump lifts nutrient water to every tier. It flows past the roots in the channels and drains back to the tank. There is no soil, and the same water goes round again.

Closed loopPump 24 h~20 W
04 Night

Growing after dark

After sunset the battery takes over. LED grow lights extend the day and the pump keeps running. Drag the clock to see any hour.

05 Cloudy days

When the clouds stay, the plants still drink

If the battery runs low, the controller switches loads off in order. Fans go first, then the lights. The pump always keeps running.

Fans off< 35%
LED lights off< 20%
Pumpalways on
06 Scale

As big and as high as you want

Modules are built like scaffolding. Bolt them side by side or stack them upward. Each one brings its own panel, battery and controller, so the farm stays solar however large it gets.

One module is about 2.1 m × 1.1 m × 1.8 m with 4 tiers (indicative).

Our journey

Three versions, each one learning from the last

We started in 2023 with a small lettuce farm that taught us what goes wrong. Version 3 is the one that runs on its own sun. It is what this raise builds.

  1. 2023v1

    First MVP

    A small hydroponic lettuce farm. A lot went wrong; we fixed what we could and kept notes on the rest.

    ~70% of the lettuce harvested
  2. 2024v2

    Vertical racks

    Stacked PVC grow channels on steel racks, indoors under LED lights and outdoors in the sun. We grew bell peppers and habaneros.

    Indoor + outdoor racks

What we do

From first visit to every harvest

One team handles the whole farm, so a client never has to understand batteries or nutrient levels to grow food.

  1. Plan

    We visit the site and size the farm: how many modules, how high, and what to grow.

  2. Build

    Scaffold frames, grow channels, solar panels and power cabinets, made in our workshop.

  3. Install

    We set it up, connect power and water, and plant the first crop with the client.

  4. Maintain

    Regular visits to check the battery, pump and nutrients, and quick fixes so it keeps producing.

  5. Train and supply

    Climate-smart farming training and nutrients matched to each crop.

Clients can install and maintain the farm themselves, but we advise against it: our team knows the solar and water systems inside out.

Where it started

It began at a vacation camp, teaching 15 children to build a hydroponic farm.

As they worked, we saw what this could become: fresh food grown close to home, in very little space. Three versions later, we are making it run entirely on the sun, so it keeps growing whatever the grid does.
